New Release

Debut Album 《Korean Dystopia》

Release Oct 28, 2026 (upcoming) Label Mirrorball Music Tracks 12 main tracks Producers MOOII, Kim Jae-hyun

The album deals with events that took place in South Korea in the early-to-mid 2020s, so understanding the social context of the time makes it more enjoyable. If you lived in Korea during this period, the lyrics will make sense on their own; for listeners who didn't, we have added notes on these events to this page, marked "Social Context."

This is not an album that digs deeply into social events or passes value judgments on them. Instead, we captured in lyrics the feelings and thoughts we had at the moment we saw a phenomenon. We didn't spend long agonizing over the lyrics. We didn't revise them, and we didn't censor them. While this album was being made, Son Chang-hyun lived as a journalist, Kim Jae-hyun as a software developer, Jeong Min-gu as a part-timer (a "freeter"), Kim Da-mi as a university student, and Kim Jun-sung as a high school student. MOOII's music arose as a by-product of these members' lives, and this album is the organic unfolding of those by-products.

Our musical references include Pixies, Pavement, Joy Division and The Strokes, but after many arguments we never found a single genre that all the members could agree on. So on each song, everyone plays what they want to play. Broadly, drummer Jeong Min-gu leans punk, guitarist Kim Jae-hyun leans post-punk revival and shoegaze, bassists Kim Jun-sung and Kim Da-mi lean post-punk, and vocalist Son Chang-hyun leans alternative and hard rock. We have listed every song we can remember referencing while writing.

Behind

In 2023, MOOII was booked for the Namwon Yocheon Beach Festival and decided to bring a song about summer. We had titled it "Blue Hawaii" as a working title and finished all the musical parts, melody included, but the lyrics wouldn't come. That summer was brutally hot and humid, and around then a series of stabbing attacks took place in Korea. We drank beer and talked about it for a long while. The next day, Son Chang-hyun brought in these lyrics. Some members objected to the content, but we had to perform it at the festival the following week, so there was no time to revise and the song came out as it was. They say it's a lyric mocking Choi Won-jong.

Social Context

In the summer of 2023, a number of high-profile stabbing attacks shocked Korea, including the Sillim Station attack and the Seohyeon Station attack. Choi Won-jong, the perpetrator of the Seohyeon Station attack and the inspiration for this lyric, had been an avid user of online communities with a pro-Japan bent. Afterwards, a culture of mocking pro-Japan users by calling them the "Won-jong gang" (원종단), after his name, took hold.

Social Context

In Korea, bull markets driven by crypto in 2022 and by semiconductors in 2026 drew in large numbers of young investors, and stock and crypto investing spread quickly, especially among men in their twenties. Among the young, a mindset took hold that you can no longer get rich through work alone, so you must save and invest while you are as young as possible. Older generations who criticize this behavior form one axis of the generational conflict. Today, almost no young office workers in their twenties and thirties are without stock investments. But with prices so volatile, some young people have become rich, while others have lost everything they had saved.

Behind

A song about "Pink Palace," a documentary dealing with the sexuality of people with disabilities. Musically, it was finished right away on the day of rehearsal: Jeong Min-gu heard Kim Jae-hyun trying out Michael Jackson's "Black or White" in a Strokes style, and asked him to loosen it up with the languor of a setting sun. That day, Oh Jun-myung was late to rehearsal because of the Solidarity Against Disability Discrimination (SADD) subway protest, which is why disability became the subject of the lyrics. Son Chang-hyun and Jeong Min-gu, who had been deeply impressed by the documentary Pink Palace, suggested naming the song "Pink Palace."

Social Context

From late 2021 to late 2023, the Solidarity Against Disability Discrimination (SADD, 전장연) kept up protests at major subway stations, blocking passengers from boarding and train doors from opening and closing, which disrupted train service, to demand a larger welfare budget for people with disabilities and the right to leave institutions (deinstitutionalization). The protests inconvenienced many citizens, became a major controversy, and turned public attitudes toward people with disabilities negative. Still, they also became the occasion for the mandatory introduction of low-floor buses and for the partial adoption of laws guaranteeing and supporting the rights of people with disabilities.

Behind

A song knocked out in rehearsal the day after Son Chang-hyun broke up with his girlfriend, born from an improvised jam. The original lyrics were rather vulgar: "You con artist bitch, how much money did I spend on you, give me my money back. You ruined my life." But after a few conversations with her, Son Chang-hyun realized he was at fault too, and immediately changed the lyrics to a confession of repentance. Below is the moment we recorded, right after the song came together.

Kim Jae-hyun's thoughts: Son Chang-hyun's ex-girlfriend was an international student, and when her WeChat Pay account was suspended* and she was in a bind, he gave her about 5 million won in financial support, including paying her tuition. After they broke up, the fact that he had lent her the money was left to fade away. The total cost of making Korean Dystopia was less than 5 million won; 5 million won is a lot of money, and not paying it back feels like a kind of fraud. I respect Chang-hyun for saying coolly that it's fine not to be paid back, but I don't understand it.

* See "Social Context" below.

Social Context

A Chinese voice-phishing ring operating in Korea was caught after using WeChat Pay as a means of currency exchange. In response, the police took the drastic measure of suspending every foreign-linked account that had attempted currency exchange via WeChat. Innocent people who had tried to exchange money through WeChat, such as international students and tourists, also had their accounts suspended, and Son Chang-hyun's ex-girlfriend was one such case.

Behind

Kim Jun-sung, MOOII's former bassist, lived in the same apartment complex as Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president of South Korea*. On the day of his impeachment, the complex descended into chaos with protesters** and police. In the middle of it, Kim Jun-sung slipped out of the complex and recorded the voices on his phone just as they were. The outcry of "The country is ruined!" in the middle of the song is that very voice.

* Yoon Suk-yeol, the 20th president of South Korea, declared martial law in December 2024 and was impeached and removed from office in 2025.

** Protesters who supported Yoon Suk-yeol.

Social Context

In Korea, voting tendencies split by generation and gender: people in their 70s and older tend to support right-wing parties, the middle-aged (40s to 60s) tend to support left-wing parties, and the younger generation tends to support right-wing parties again. Men mostly support right-wing parties, and women mostly support left-wing ones. Behind this lie the historical context of Korea's rise from an undeveloped country, through a developing one, to an advanced one, as well as factors such as men's military service.
